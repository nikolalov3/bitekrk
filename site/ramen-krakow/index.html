The Best Ramen and Japanese in Krakow

By Cezary Musiał · Published 23 July 2026 · Addresses and prices checked August 2026

Krakow has quietly become a good ramen town. Not a Tokyo-good one, but a city where broths simmer for hours, noodles are pulled in house, and two bars trade the top spot on the local rankings by a whisker. Add a sushi counter that has been in the Michelin Guide for over a decade and a charcoal robata grill, and you can eat Japanese here across the whole range, from a 44 zloty bowl to a wagyu skewer.

A note on the honors. Two of the six below are in the Michelin Guide selection, which means recommended, not a star and not a Bib Gourmand. The ramen bars have no awards, they earn their place on the broth. Below, three ramen counters, two sushi rooms and one robata grill, roughly best to different.

Kazimierz · ramen

Akita Ramen

The City of Krakow's own guide puts Akita first, and the locals agree, usually adding "competition far behind". It grew out of a food truck, so the room is small, informal and counter-led, with no reservations and a queue at peak. The reason is the tonkotsu: a genuinely thick, collagen-heavy pork broth that clings to the noodles. Order it plain first, then argue about whether the spicy Red Alert tantan is even better.

ul. Węgłowa 4, Kazimierz Tue to Sun, closed Mondays, no reservations $$
9.5/10
What to order
Tonkotsu ramen + the pork-skin side

Ranked the city's best ramen by Krakow's official guide. The tonkotsu is thick and collagen-rich in a way most places here cannot match. Come off-peak or queue.

Piasek · ramen

Kinki Ramen

A few minutes from the Main Square, Kinki is the best all-rounder and the official number two. The noodles are made on site, and they run a creamy chicken-and-pork paitan alongside a clean shoyu, so both broth camps are covered. The flagship Hot'n'Kinki miso comes loaded with crispy kara-age chicken. If you want one ramen bar that is central, reliable and does everything well, this is it.

ul. Piłsudskiego 1, Piasek Daily, lunch to evening $$
9/10
What to order
Hot'n'Kinki miso ramen + kara-age

In-house noodles, both paitan and clear shoyu done well, a couple of minutes from the Rynek. The safest central choice, and the miso with crispy chicken is the one to beat.

Piasek · ramen

Ramen People

The most-reviewed Japanese place in the city and the broth-nerd favorite, running since 2017. The menu is built around two broth families: assari, the long-cooked light clear chicken, and kotteri, the collagen-rich pork. Broths simmer for hours, noodles are made fresh daily, and there is a second branch across the river in Podgorze. If you are feeding a group and want a sure thing, start here.

9/10

Asakusa Shoyu + gyoza to start

The most-reviewed Japanese kitchen in Krakow, split cleanly between light assari and rich kotteri broths. Consistent enough to be the group-dinner default.

Kazimierz · sushi

Hana Sushi

Krakow's benchmark for traditional sushi, on Kupa in Kazimierz, and in the Michelin Guide selection for roughly eleven years running, recommended rather than starred. The sushi is prepared by a master chef trained in Tokyo, with premium fish, bluefin tuna, Scottish salmon, sea bass, cut to order rather than pre-made. Come for the chef's set or the aburi cuts, or a weekday lunch bento if you want the quality without the full spend.

Kazimierz · omakase

Youmiko Sushi

The most inventive sushi in Krakow, a small omakase counter on the edge of Kazimierz where the chef torches and finishes each piece in front of you. Youmiko refuses frozen imported fish, leaning on fresh, local and seasonal produce and merging Japanese technique with Slavic ideas. It is also the best sushi in town for vegans. The room is tiny, so book, and sit at the counter to talk to the chef.

The omakase tasting menu at the counter

No frozen fish, fresh and local only, torched and finished in front of you. The city's most creative sushi, and its best vegan sushi too. Counter seats are the ones to book.

Old Town · robata

Nami Beef and Reef

The upscale pick, and something different from the ramen counters: a polished izakaya and robatayaki on Stolarska, cooking over an open charcoal robata grill in a dark-wood room built around a large aquarium. This is beef and skewers, wagyu over the flame, yakitori, wagyu tataki, tempura, with nigiri and rolls to round it out. Also in the Michelin Guide selection. Come for grilled small plates and sake rather than a bowl of soup.

Charcoal robata, wagyu and sake in a dark room around an aquarium, also in the Michelin Guide selection. The grown-up Japanese night out when you do not want ramen.

How to use this list

Craving a bowl, go straight to Akita for tonkotsu or Kinki for the central all-rounder, and take Ramen People for a group. Want sushi, Hana for the traditional Michelin-listed counter, Youmiko for the creative omakase and the best vegan option. Want a proper night out, book Nami and eat off the charcoal grill. Most of the ramen bars close Mondays, so plan around it.

The Japanese scene here clusters hard in Kazimierz, with Akita, Hana and Youmiko within a few streets, then spreads to the Old Town edge (Kinki, Ramen People, Nami). If you cross the river, Ramen People's Podgorze branch and a couple of Zablocie spots are extending the map.

Questions people actually ask

Which is better, Akita or Kinki?

Akita for the thickest, most serious tonkotsu, and it tops the city's official ranking, but it takes no bookings and queues. Kinki is more central, more of an all-rounder with in-house noodles, and easier to get into. Both are excellent.

Where is the best sushi in Krakow?

Hana Sushi for traditional, a Tokyo-trained chef and about eleven years in the Michelin Guide selection. Youmiko for creative omakase with no frozen fish and the best vegan sushi. Nami if you want a robata grill rather than a sushi bar.

Is Japanese food in Krakow expensive?

Ramen is good value at 42 to 52 zloty a bowl. Sushi and robata climb: expect 100 zloty and up per person at Hana, Youmiko or Nami. A ramen dinner with a drink stays around 60 to 75 zloty.
