Best brunch in Krakow: six spots for a slow weekend

By Cezary Musiał · Published 29 August 2026 · Details checked August 2026 · How we test

Brunch and breakfast are not the same meal, whatever the menus say. Breakfast in Krakow is early and quick, eggs and bread on the way to somewhere. Brunch is the weekend version: later, longer, a larger plate, a second coffee, and no reason to leave the table for two hours. This guide is the second kind. Six places where a Saturday late morning is the whole plan, ranked by how good the food is once you factor in the room you eat it in. For the early, quick version, our breakfast guide is the one you want.

01

Kolanko No 6

The Kazimierz brunch institution, and the one most locals name first. Kolanko has been doing crepes, big breakfast plates and a weekend spread on ul. Józefa for years, in a long room that opens onto a vine-covered courtyard. The garden is the reason to come and the reason it fills by noon on a Sunday, so arrive early or plan to wait with a coffee. Bococa Bistro

For brunch that leans modern bistro rather than cafe, Bococa on Plac Inwalidów in Krowodrza is the pick. This is where to go for a proper eggs benedict, a real hollandaise, and a calm room a short walk north-west of the Old Town, away from the weekend crush. It is pet friendly and the kind of neighborhood place locals keep to themselves. The Krowodrza guide has the rest of the area.

Ambalaz Cafe

The highest rated place on this list, and the one with the view. Ambalaz sits on top of the Cricoteka art centre on the Podgorze riverbank, with a rooftop terrace that looks straight back across the Vistula at Wawel and Kazimierz. All-day breakfast means you can brunch here at any hour, and the setting does half the work. Pair it with the art downstairs and our Podgorze guide.

Charlotte

The cheapest good brunch in the city, and the earliest croissants. Charlotte is a French bakery-cafe on plac Szczepański, one of the sunniest squares on the Old Town edge, baking its own bread overnight in the basement. How to plan a brunch morning

The two districts to base a brunch around are Kazimierz and the Old Town edge, both walkable and both full of options if your first choice has a queue. If the weather is good, Ambalaz across the river is worth the twenty minute walk for the terrace alone, and you can pair it with the art at Cricoteka and MOCAK downstairs. Book the popular rooms on weekends, come before eleven, and leave room for a second coffee. See how it all connects on the food map, or browse every place we cover.

Questions people actually ask

What is the difference between breakfast and brunch in Krakow?

Breakfast spots open early and serve quick eggs, bread and coffee from around 7 or 8am, for people on their way somewhere. Brunch is the weekend, sit-down version: later, slower, larger plates like eggs benedict, buffets and crepes, often with a garden. This guide covers brunch. For the early, quick version, see our breakfast guide.

Where is the best brunch in Krakow?

For a classic Krakow brunch, Kolanko No 6 on ul. Józefa 17 in Kazimierz is the institution, with crepes, a weekend spread and a courtyard garden. For modern brunch with proper eggs benedict, Bococa Bistro in Krowodrza. For the best view, Ambalaz on top of the Cricoteka art centre in Podgorze, which is also the highest rated.

How much does brunch cost in Krakow?

A sit-down brunch with a main plate and coffee runs roughly 35 to 60 PLN per person, about 9 to 15 USD. Bakery-cafes like Charlotte sit at the lower end, while a full weekend spread with several plates and drinks climbs toward 70 to 90 PLN.

Do I need to book brunch in Krakow?

On weekends, yes for the popular rooms. Kolanko No 6 and Ambalaz fill by late morning on Saturday and Sunday, so book or arrive before 11am. Charlotte, Ranny Ptaszek and Veganic are more walk-in friendly, though you may wait for a garden table in summer.
