Krakow for Digital Nomads: Where to Work, What It Costs

By Cezary Musiał · Published 14 August 2026 · Details checked August 2026

Krakow has everything a remote worker needs and charges half of what Western Europe asks for it. Home broadband averages 250 Mbps, a coworking day pass costs 40 to 50 PLN, a one-bedroom apartment in the center runs 3,000 to 3,500 PLN a month, and a business lunch is 25 to 35 PLN. The city is compact, walkable, well-connected by cheap trams, and has a deep enough cafe and restaurant scene that you will not get bored in six months.

This guide covers three things: cafes where a laptop is genuinely welcome, coworking spaces with real pricing, and the practical numbers you need to plan a stay. If you are already here and just want to know where to open a laptop today, start with our work-friendly cafes guide or the quiet cafes list.

Cafes for working

Not every cafe with wifi is a good place to work. These three are actively laptop-friendly: stable wifi, enough outlets, reasonable noise, and staff who do not mind you staying for three hours on two coffees.

01

Bruk Cafe

In the Kleparz neighborhood, north of the Old Town, Bruk is one of the quietest specialty cafes in the city and one of the most genuinely laptop-friendly. The room is calm, the wifi is stable, the coffee is serious, and the location puts you between the train station and Planty park without the tourist noise of either. It opens at ten on weekdays, closes at five, and attracts the kind of crowd that comes to actually get things done. If you work better in a neighborhood cafe than in a coworking space, this is your answer.

Massolit Books & Cafe

West of the Old Town on a quiet street off Planty, Massolit is a bookshop and cafe in one, with 20,000 English-language titles on the shelves and a reading-room hush that makes it one of the best deep-work spots in the city. The wifi is solid, long stays are welcome, and the cakes are good enough that you will buy one around three in the afternoon whether you planned to or not. 03

Cytat Cafe

In Kazimierz on Miodowa street, Cytat is the cafe that explicitly markets itself to laptop workers and gets the details right: plenty of power outlets at the tables, fast free wifi, long opening hours (until 21:00 on weekdays, 22:00 on weekends), and a relaxed literary atmosphere with sofas and regular tables. The breakfast is first-class, the coffee is solid, and the hours mean you can work a full day here and still be in Kazimierz for dinner. If you need a Kazimierz base, this is it.

Coworking spaces

When you need a monitor, a phone booth for a call, or just a full work day without ordering a third coffee out of guilt, these three coworking spaces offer day passes and monthly plans at a fraction of what you would pay in London or Berlin.

04

Yolk Workspace

The highest-rated coworking space in Krakow and the most complete. Gigabit fiber with a backup connection, acoustic phone booths, a podcast studio, standing desks, 24/7 access, and a specialty cafe on the ground floor. It feels like a well-funded startup office rather than a generic hot-desk farm. The community runs climbing trips and sports groups, which is either a plus or a warning depending on your personality. A day pass runs about 130 PLN, a monthly hot desk about 650 PLN.

05

Cluster Cowork

The most established coworking brand in the city, with four locations across Krakow. The main space at Plac Inwalidow has high ceilings, tall windows, private offices, conference rooms and showers for cyclists. A day pass is about 50 PLN, a monthly hot desk starts at 399 PLN, and you get unlimited coffee, 24/7 access and a community that mixes Polish startups with international freelancers. The price-to-quality ratio is hard to beat. Pet-friendly, which in a coworking space is either charming or distracting.

Coworking Rynek 28

A coworking space on the Main Market Square. That is the pitch, and it works. The building is from the 16th century, the views face the Town Hall Tower, the day pass is 50 PLN, and they offer a free trial day so you can see if the location is worth the occasional tourist noise drifting up from below. Twenty ergonomic workstations, two meeting rooms, included coffee and tea. If you are in Krakow for a week and want to work from the most photogenic office in the city, this is it.

The numbers

Monthly costs

A comfortable month in Krakow costs roughly 5,500 to 7,500 PLN (1,300 to 1,800 USD). Here is the breakdown:

  • Rent, 1BR city center: 3,000 to 3,500 PLN (730 to 850 USD)
  • Groceries + dining: 1,500 to 2,000 PLN (365 to 490 USD)
  • Coworking hot desk: 400 to 650 PLN (100 to 160 USD)
  • Transport pass: 109 PLN (27 USD)
  • Internet + mobile: 95 to 100 PLN (23 to 24 USD)
  • Utilities share: 500 to 700 PLN (120 to 170 USD)

For context: Krakow is about 30 percent cheaper than Warsaw and roughly half the cost of Berlin or Amsterdam. A business lunch runs 25 to 35 PLN. A specialty coffee is 12 to 18 PLN. A craft beer in a bar is about 16 PLN.

Internet

Home broadband averages 250 Mbps down, 90 Mbps up with 19 ms latency. Mobile data averages 180 Mbps on 5G. Fiber connections of 100 to 300 Mbps are standard in central apartments. Video calls, screen shares and large file uploads are never a problem. Orange is the fastest broadband provider, T-Mobile leads on mobile.

Visas

EU/EEA citizens need nothing. Live and work indefinitely, register residence after three months.

Non-EU citizens from visa-waiver countries (US, UK, Canada, Australia and others) get 90 days visa-free under Schengen rules. Remote work for a foreign employer is fine. For longer stays, a national D visa (up to one year) or a temporary residence permit (up to three years, requires proof of income and health insurance) are the main paths. Poland does not yet have a dedicated digital nomad visa.

Where to live

Kazimierz is the top pick for most nomads: busy, walkable, full of cafes and bars, with a large international community. Rents are slightly above average but the atmosphere earns it. Podgorze across the river is the budget-friendly alternative with a growing food scene (see our Podgorze guide). Krowodrza is greener and quieter, close to Cluster coworking. Stare Miasto is beautiful but touristy and expensive. Debniki near the university campus is affordable and improving.

The pattern

The typical nomad week in Krakow: work from a cafe two or three days (Bruk for deep focus, Cytat for late hours, Massolit when you need the bookshop quiet), coworking one or two days for calls and meetings (Cluster for value, Yolk for the full setup), and the rest exploring a city that has more to eat and drink than you will cover in a month. Our breakfast guide, specialty coffee map and Kazimierz guide are good places to start when you close the laptop.

Also worth knowing: Kalafiornia (ul. Sarego 18) is a no-frills deep-work coworking with a rooftop terrace and 40 PLN day passes, deliberately quiet with no events or community distractions. And MOOD (ul. Klimeckiego 14b) is a specialty cafe with a mezzanine and hours until 23:00, which solves the problem of most Krakow cafes closing at five or six.
