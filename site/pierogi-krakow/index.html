The Best Pierogi in Krakow, and the Polish Food Around Them

By Cezary Musiał · Published 9 July 2026 · Addresses and prices checked August 2026

Every visitor to Krakow asks the same question, and most get the wrong answer. The pierogi places on the Main Square charge two to three times the going rate, often for dumplings boiled from frozen, while the places Poles actually queue for sit five to fifteen minutes away on side streets. This list is those places: an eighty-year-old lunch canteen, a hand-folding open kitchen, a real communist-era milk bar, and, at the far end of the scale, the only two-Michelin-star kitchen in Poland.

One honest note before the list. Pierogi are lunch food here, and the most loved spots keep lunch hours and sell out. Plan them for the middle of the day, keep an evening backup, and treat a queue of locals as the best recommendation Krakow can give you.

Old Town · the legend

Jadłodajnia U Stasi

A family canteen in a courtyard off Mikołajska that has been feeding Krakow for roughly eighty years. No reservations, a queue that forms before the noon opening, and a handwritten menu where dishes get crossed off as they sell out, often by mid-afternoon. Order the fruit pierogi in season, blueberry or plum, and eat what three generations of this city grew up on. Weekday lunch only.

ul. Mikołajska 16, Old Town Weekday lunch, roughly 12:00 to 17:00, sells out $
9.5/10
What to order
Seasonal fruit pierogi + pierogi ruskie

Eighty years of practice, a queue of locals before noon, and dishes crossed off a handwritten menu as they go. The pierogi benchmark in this city, full stop.

Wesoła · hand-folded

Przystanek Pierogarnia

The best all-day alternative, on Bonerowska near Hala Targowa at the Old Town's eastern edge. A small room with an open kitchen where the dough is rolled and the dumplings folded by hand in front of you, which is the single most reassuring sight a pierogi place can offer. Ruskie and wild mushroom with cabbage are the anchors, strawberry or blueberry join in summer. Closed Sundays.

ul. Bonerowska 14, Wesoła Mon to Fri 11:00 to 20:00, Sat to 17:00, closed Sun $
9/10
What to order
Wild mushroom and cabbage pierogi + summer fruit pierogi

Dough rolled and folded by hand in open view, which tells you everything before the plate arrives. The reliable all-day pierogi in the center.

Kazimierz · 30 fillings

Pierogi Mr Vincent

A tiny Van Gogh-themed room on Bożego Ciała in Kazimierz, sunflowers and Starry Night on the walls, and a menu of about thirty fillings that runs from minced lamb to chicken and spinach to combinations like apple and liver that sound wrong and eat right. Open daily until nine, which makes it the pierogi dinner option this list otherwise lacks.

ul. Bożego Ciała 12, Kazimierz Daily 11:00 to 21:00 $
8.5/10
What to order
Minced lamb pierogi + sweet fruit pierogi with cream

Thirty fillings in a room the size of a train compartment, painted like a Van Gogh. The evening pierogi answer in Kazimierz, and the adventurous one.

Royal Route · milk bar

Bar mleczny Pod Temidą

A surviving communist-era milk bar on Grodzka, halfway between the Main Square and Wawel, which is prime tourist land occupied by a place charging under twenty zloty for most mains. Order at the counter, pay upfront, carry your tray, share a table. Pierogi ruskie, żurek, potato pancakes and a glass of kompot: the full Polish canteen experience at the address where it should be impossible.

ul. Grodzka 43, Old Town Daily from 9:00 until about 20:00 $
8.5/10

Old Town · white tablecloth

Restauracja Pod Baranem

When you want old-school Polish cooking served properly, this is the address: family-run since 1997 by chef-owner Jan Baran, five homely carpeted rooms hung with contemporary art, facing the Planty near Wawel. Żurek, roast duck with apples, beef roulade with buckwheat. It appeared in earlier editions of the Michelin Guide, and though it is not in the current selection, the cooking has not moved an inch, which is exactly the point of it.

ul. św. Gertrudy 21, Old Town Lunch and dinner daily $$
9/10

Kazimierz · two stars

Bottiglieria 1881

The other end of Polish food entirely. In an 1881 former wine warehouse on quiet Bocheńska in Kazimierz, chef Przemysław Klima cooks refined modern Polish as a tasting menu only, above a cellar of about 1,600 bottles that runs deepest in Champagne and Burgundy. It holds two stars in the 2026 Michelin Guide Poland and was the first restaurant in Poland ever to earn a second star. Book weeks ahead, come hungry, and see what this cuisine can be.

How to use this list

One pierogi meal in Krakow: U Stasi, weekday, just before noon. Evening pierogi: Mr Vincent in Kazimierz. The cheapest real Polish lunch on the tourist route: Pod Temidą. A proper sit-down Polish dinner: Pod Baranem. And if the trip deserves one blowout, Bottiglieria 1881 is the best restaurant in Poland by the only ranking that counts stars.

A newer name worth watching: Słońce on św. Tomasza, a modern Polish opening with hand-rolled pierogi in open view, which we covered when it opened and will score after the kitchen settles.

Questions people actually ask

What are pierogi ruskie?

The classic filling: potato, twaróg cheese and fried onion. The name means "Ruthenian", not Russian. It is the default order across Poland and the benchmark to judge any pierogi kitchen by.

Why do the best pierogi places close so early?

Because pierogi are lunch food, made fresh in the morning and sold until they run out. U Stasi often sells out by mid-afternoon. Plan pierogi for lunch and Polish dinners at Pod Baranem or Mr Vincent.

How much should pierogi cost?

At an honest kitchen, a plate runs about 20 to 35 zloty. On the Main Square you will see 50 to 70 for a worse plate. The milk bar keeps most mains under 20.
