Podgorze: 6 Reasons to Cross the River

By Cezary Musiał · Published 14 August 2026 · Details checked August 2026

Most visitors reach Podgorze for one reason: Schindler's Factory. They cross the river, spend two hours in the museum, and walk straight back. The district barely gets a chance. That is a mistake, because the food scene on this side of the Vistula is one of the most interesting in Krakow right now, with three Michelin-recommended restaurants, a French bakery run by an actual French-Belgian couple, and a cafe view that nothing on the Main Square can touch.

A word on geography. Podgorze sits south of the Vistula, connected to Kazimierz by the Kladka Bernatka footbridge. The district has three clusters worth knowing: Rynek Podgorski, the old market square where Mazi, Vamos and Nad & Greg sit within a minute of each other; the Nadwislanska riverside, where Cricoteka, Ambalaz and Drukarnia line the water; and Zablocie, the post-industrial quarter around Schindler's Factory and MOCAK, where Hankki is your best lunch option. You can walk all three in an afternoon, eat very well at each stop, and spend 20 to 30 percent less than you would in the Old Town.

01

Vamos!

A side street off Rynek Podgorski, a bright room, and chef Ada Wojcikowska building one of the most exciting menus in the city. Vamos earned a Michelin recommendation in 2025 and it is easy to see why: the sharing plates are precise without being fussy, the pintxos in the deli case at the entrance change daily, and the focaccia alone would justify crossing the river. The duck fillet with arrabbiata lentils is the dish people come back for. Book for dinner on weekends or arrive early for the counter seats.

Three courses of sharing plates for 140 PLN. Every dish had a clear idea, none outstayed its welcome. The tiramisu is dangerously good.

02

Mazi

Directly on Rynek Podgorski, in the kind of spot that in the Old Town would be overpriced and underwhelming, Mazi does the opposite: honest Mediterranean sharing plates, generous olive oil, and prices that make you double-check the bill. The pitta, baked fresh and served with salt and herbs, arrives before you have finished ordering and sets the tone for everything after it. In summer the outdoor tables face the square's lime trees and the Podgorze parish church. Another Michelin recommendation, another reason the south bank is pulling ahead.

Rynek Podgorski 9 Lunch and dinner, outdoor seating in summer $$
9/10
What I ordered
Fresh pitta + sharing plates + cannoli

The pitta is warm, torn-not-cut good. Sharing plates are built to pick at slowly with wine. Cannoli finish the job.

03

Ambalaz Cafe

Take the lift to the fourth floor of Cricoteka, the Tadeusz Kantor art centre, and walk into the best cafe view in Krakow. Floor-to-ceiling windows frame the Vistula, the Bernatka footbridge, and the Kazimierz rooftops in a single panorama that no Main Square terrace can match. The coffee is specialty grade, the French toast with kogel-mogel is unreasonably good for a museum cafe, and from two in the afternoon pierogi ruskie join the menu. Come for breakfast, stay for the view, leave wondering why you spent three days on the other side of the river.

ul. Nadwislanska 2/4, Cricoteka 4th floor Daily 10:00 to 19:00 $$
8.5/10
What I ordered
French toast with kogel-mogel + flat white

Crispy outside, custardy inside, the kogel-mogel adds richness without sweetness overload. The flat white is clean and balanced. The view does the rest.

04

Hankki

Zablocie used to be a blank spot on the food map. You visited Schindler's Factory, maybe MOCAK, and then walked back to Kazimierz to eat. Hankki changed that. A Michelin-recommended Korean-Japanese kitchen in a bright, blonde-wood room on ul. Zablocie, five minutes from the museum. The red jjamppong, a spicy seafood noodle soup, is the kind of bowl that makes you forget about the cold in November. The popcorn chicken is dangerously moreish. Lunch bento boxes are excellent value. If you are visiting Schindler's Factory, this is where you eat before or after.

ul. Zablocie 19A Lunch and dinner $$
8.5/10
What I ordered
Red jjamppong + popcorn chicken

Properly spicy broth, generous seafood, noodles with the right chew. The popcorn chicken is the side dish that steals the meal.

05

Nad & Greg

On the quieter side of Rynek Podgorski, a French-Belgian couple bake every croissant, eclair and macaron by hand from seasonal ingredients. The croissants are large and laminated with a patience you can taste, the pain au chocolat is the real thing, and the seasonal fruit eclairs change with whatever is ripe. It is a small operation, unpretentious, and the kind of morning ritual Podgorze locals guard carefully. Pair a croissant with coffee on the square and you have the cheapest great breakfast in this guide at under 25 PLN.

Rynek Podgorski 11 Mon to Thu 7:30 to 20:00, Fri to Sun 8:30 to 20:00 $
9/10
What I ordered
Croissant + pain au chocolat + coffee

The croissant shatters properly, which tells you everything. The pain au chocolat is the best in Krakow by a margin nobody else is closing.

06

Drukarnia

On the Vistula embankment, next door to Cricoteka, Drukarnia is two venues in one. During the day it is a relaxed riverside cafe with large windows, good coffee and weekend brunch. After dark the basement becomes a jazz club with live concerts, stand-up and the kind of atmosphere that keeps you out later than you planned. The food leans tapas and international, the craft beer list is solid, and the terrace in summer faces the river and the Kazimierz skyline. If your Podgorze day started at Nad & Greg with a croissant, this is a good place to end it.

ul. Nadwislanska 1 Daily from 9:00, Fri to Sat until 4:00 $$

Weekend brunch + craft IPA on the terrace

Reliable brunch, the view does heavy lifting, and the jazz in the basement later is the surprise bonus. Not the most refined food, but the right food in the right place.

The pattern

Podgorze rewards a specific kind of visit: cross the Bernatka footbridge from Kazimierz, start with coffee and a croissant at Nad & Greg on the square, walk south to the Cricoteka building for the view from Ambalaz, then loop through Zablocie for Hankki if you are visiting Schindler's Factory. Come back to Rynek Podgorski for dinner at Vamos or Mazi, and end at Drukarnia if the jazz programme looks good. The whole circuit is walkable in an afternoon and evening, and you will eat better and spend less than you would following the same clock on the north side of the river.

If Podgorze is the third Krakow district you are exploring after our Kleparz and Kazimierz guides, the contrast tells you something about the city: Kleparz feeds the neighborhood, Kazimierz performs for the crowd, and Podgorze quietly collects Michelin recommendations while nobody is looking. The trick, as always, is crossing the river.

Three more places worth noting if you have time: Euskadi (ul. Brodzinskiego 4), a Michelin-recommended Basque restaurant next door to Vamos, for a different take on Spanish cooking. Kuchnia Polska Gaska (ul. Limanowskiego 1), opposite the square, for serious Polish home cooking built around goose dishes. And Plac Bohaterow Getta, the haunting memorial with empty-chair sculptures, a short walk from Rynek Podgorski, which is not food but is something you should see.
