The Best Indian Food in Krakow

By Cezary Musiał · Published 8 August 2026 · Addresses and prices checked August 2026

Indian food in Krakow has quietly gotten very good and stayed very cheap. The six below cover the whole map: the current highest-rated North Indian in the city, the only real South Indian dosa kitchen in Poland, the butter chicken the awards keep going to, and a fully vegan sattvic room that a lot of non-vegans book anyway. Most of it clusters on one street on the edge of Kazimierz, which locals treat as curry row.

A note on the range. There is no fine-dining Indian in Krakow, and prices reflect it: a proper dinner for two with a drink rarely passes ninety zloty a head, and the budget end starts under twenty. Spice defaults to mild for Polish palates, so if you want it hot, say so and most kitchens will oblige. Six places, best to different.

Old Town · North Indian

Indian Flame Wielopole

The current crowd favorite and breakout name, on Wielopole in the eastern Old Town. Modern halal North Indian with famously generous portions, sitting around 4.9 stars across more than a thousand reviews and ranked in the top fifteen of every Krakow restaurant, not just the Indian ones. Order the butter chicken or the tikka masala with cheese naan and understand why the queue formed.

Old Town · tandoor

Tandoori Flame

The most decorated Indian in Krakow for sheer consistency, on Zwierzyniecka near the Planty on the Wawel side. A clay-tandoor kitchen turning out the city's benchmark butter chicken and a proper mixed grill, with generous portions and real value. It has taken the TripAdvisor Travellers' Choice award five years running, which is the clearest signal there is that a kitchen does not have off nights.

Podgórze · South Indian

Madras Bistro

The most interesting Indian in Krakow and the only one cooking the coastal south: Poland's oldest dedicated South Indian restaurant, across the river on Plac Bohaterów Getta. Chef Victor is from Kerala and makes dosa, uttapam, parotta and sambar rather than the usual cream-heavy North Indian menu. The room has about three tables, so it is tiny, but the masala dosa and the bestselling masala uttapam are worth the trip and the wait.

Plac Bohaterów Getta 2, Podgórze Tue to Sun, closed Mondays, tiny room $$
9/10
What to order
Masala dosa + masala uttapam

Poland's oldest South Indian kitchen, a Kerala chef cooking dosa and uttapam instead of the usual curries. Three tables, so it fills, but it is the only place in the city doing this properly.

Stradom · vegetarian

Bhajan Cafe

The city's standout for plant-based Indian, on Stradomska near Wawel: a strictly vegetarian and vegan sattvic kitchen with extensive vegan and gluten-free options. Paneer makhani, palak paneer or its vegan tofu version, chilli kofta, samosas, tandoori paneer. The venue also has a meditation cave and hosts yoga and concerts, which sounds like a lot, but the food stands on its own at 4.8 stars. A favorite well beyond the vegan crowd.

Kazimierz · curry row

Taste of India

A long-running classic on Dietla, the street locals treat as curry row, and the place that lets you set your own spice across three levels. Order a chicken or lamb biryani or a curry at the heat you actually want, with strong gluten-free and vegetarian marking. There is a larger sister restaurant, Gate of India, on Stradomska if you are feeding a group.

Kazimierz · budget halal

Zayka

Two doors down from Taste of India on Dietla, Zayka is the budget and halal pick: a fully halal-certified tandoor kitchen, open more than a decade, with mains starting around sixteen zloty. Butter chicken, biryani, tandoori, dal and fresh naan, done honestly and cheaply. At 4.7 stars it is one of the highest-rated Indian spots in the city, and the best-value plate of the six.

How to use this list

For the current best all-round dinner, Indian Flame Wielopole or, for the safest consistency, Tandoori Flame. For something you cannot get anywhere else in the city, Madras Bistro's dosa across the river in Podgorze. For plant-based, Bhajan Cafe. And for a cheap, reliable curry, walk down Dietla on the edge of Kazimierz, where Taste of India and Zayka sit almost next door to each other.

A couple of notes for the deeper list. Hariprasad Samosa and Curry on Dietla is a tiny budget vegan and ayurvedic cult spot, and Gate of India on Stradomska is Taste of India's larger sister for groups. One name to skip for now: Indus Tandoor shows conflicting closure signals, so we have left it off until it is confirmed open.

Questions people actually ask

Where is the best butter chicken in Krakow?

Tandoori Flame on Zwierzyniecka is the consensus benchmark, out of a clay tandoor, and Indian Flame Wielopole runs it close. Both are North Indian kitchens that take the dish seriously rather than treating it as a default.

Can I get South Indian dosa in Krakow?

Yes, but at one place: Madras Bistro in Podgorze, Poland's oldest dedicated South Indian kitchen, with a Kerala chef making masala dosa, uttapam and parotta. It is tiny, about three tables, so expect a wait at peak.

Is Indian food in Krakow cheap?

Yes. Mains are usually 30 to 55 zloty and a full meal with a drink lands around 60 to 90 zloty. Zayka, fully halal on Dietla, starts around sixteen zloty a main. There is no fine-dining Indian in the city to push prices up.
