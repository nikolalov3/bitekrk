Krakow Christmas Market 2026: What to Eat and Drink

By Cezary Musiał · Published 28 August 2026 · 2026 dates expected, not yet announced by the organiser · Details checked August 2026 · How we test

For five weeks over winter, the Main Market Square fills with wooden stalls, the smell of grilling cheese and mulled wine, and the best street eating of the Krakow year. The Christmas market is a tourist magnet, but unlike most things aimed at visitors, the food is genuinely good and genuinely local. This is what to order, when to go, and how to eat well without queueing for the wrong stall.

When and where

The market runs on Rynek Główny, the Main Market Square, from the end of November 2026, around the 28th, through to 1 January 2027. Stalls open roughly 10:00 to 20:00 on weekdays, stay open until 22:00 on Friday and Saturday, and start at 11:00 on Sunday. Go on a weekday afternoon if you want to actually move; weekend evenings in December are shoulder to shoulder. The exact opening day is announced by the city closer to the date, so check before a late-November trip.

What to eat and drink

Order in roughly this sequence and you will have eaten the market properly.

Grzaniec galicyjski (mulled wine)

The drink that runs the whole market: red wine mulled with cinnamon, cloves and orange, served hot in a ceramic mug you pay a small deposit on and get back when you return the cup. There is a mead version and a non-alcoholic hot chocolate or spiced apple juice for anyone skipping the wine. This is your hand-warmer for the rest of the walk.

Grilled oscypek with cranberry

The single most Krakow thing on the square: oscypek, a smoked sheep cheese from the Tatra mountains, grilled until it blisters and served hot with a spoon of tart cranberry. Salty, smoky, sweet, and vegetarian. If you eat one thing at the market, eat this.

Kiełbasa and grilled meats

Thick grilled kiełbasa sausage on a slab of rye bread with mustard is the classic savoury bite, alongside smoked pork knuckle and the occasional ox on a spit for the theatre of it. The queues at the busiest grill stalls tell you which ones locals rate.

Pierogi, żurek and bigos

The warm-you-through trio. Pierogi come boiled or pan-fried, ruskie or meat; żurek, a sour rye soup with sausage and egg, arrives in a hollowed bread bowl you eat afterwards; and bigos, the hunter's stew of sauerkraut and meats, is the deepest, most warming thing on the square. For the pierogi that locals actually rate year-round, see our best pierogi in Krakow guide.

Pączki, obwarzanki and gingerbread

Finish sweet. Pączki are Polish doughnuts, usually filled with rose-petal jam; obwarzanki are the chewy ring-shaped pretzels sold across Krakow all year but best warm here; and decorated gingerbread hearts, pierniki, are as much a souvenir as a snack. Roasted chestnuts and sugared almonds fill in the gaps between stalls.

Tips from people who go every year

  • Cash helps. Many stalls take contactless now, but coins move the queue faster, and the mug deposit is easier in cash.
  • Weekday afternoons are calm; Friday and Saturday nights in December are the busiest hours of the Krakow year.
  • Dress for standing still. You will be outside, not walking, so one layer more than you think.
  • Escape the cold nearby. When you need to sit down and thaw, the cafes in our Old Town guide are two minutes off the square, and a proper Polish dinner is a short walk in any direction.

Where to warm up around the market

The market is for grazing; for a sit-down meal, step off the square. Traditional Polish and pierogi are covered in our pierogi guide, the quiet cafes locals use are in the Old Town guide, and Muslim visitors will find certified kitchens minutes away in our halal guide. Everything is walkable from the Rynek, as our food map shows.

Questions visitors actually ask

When does the Krakow Christmas Market open in 2026?

From the end of November 2026, around 28 November, until 1 January 2027, on the Main Market Square. Weekday hours are roughly 10:00 to 20:00, later on Friday and Saturday. The city confirms the exact opening day closer to the time.

What is the mulled wine called?

Grzaniec galicyjski, red wine mulled with cinnamon, cloves and orange, served hot in a deposit mug. There is a mead version and a non-alcoholic hot chocolate or spiced apple for anyone not drinking.

Is the market food good for vegetarians?

Yes. Grilled oscypek cheese, potato-and-cheese pierogi, roasted chestnuts, obwarzanki and pączki are all meat-free. For halal, the meat is not certified, so keep to vegetarian stalls and hot chocolate, or eat at a halal kitchen nearby.
