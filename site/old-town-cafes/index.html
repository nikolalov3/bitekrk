Old Town

Old Town Krakow Cafes That Locals Actually Use

By Cezary Musiał · Published 16 August 2026 · Updated 22 October 2026 · Hours checked October 2026 · How we test

The Old Town has more than two hundred cafes. About a hundred and ninety of them exist to serve you a mediocre latte on an overpriced terrace while you stare at the Cloth Hall. They are not hard to find. Walk toward the Main Square and let the menu-waving staff do the rest.

This guide is about the other ten percent: places where the person next to you lives here, the prices do not double because you can see a church, and the coffee was chosen by someone who drinks it. Five of them sit inside the Planty ring or at its edge. The first is a five-minute walk north of the Barbakan, in Kleparz, which still belongs to the Old Town district. All of them are close enough to the sights that you do not need to choose between a good cafe and a tourist day.

01

Bruk Cafe

Start just outside the walls. Bruk sits on Krótka, a side street between the Barbakan and the Stary Kleparz market, five minutes north of the Old Town gates and still inside the Old Town district. It is where we send people who want proper specialty coffee before the sightseeing starts: serious espresso, a matcha list longer than in most matcha bars, breakfast until 1 pm, and a Basque cheesecake that does not last long on the plate.

It also fixes the two things the cafes around the Main Square rarely get right. It opens at 8:00 on weekdays and stays open on Sunday mornings, and there are sockets within reach of most tables. The Floriańska gate is a short walk south and the Main Square about ten minutes on foot, so it works as the first stop of the day rather than a detour.

02

Nowa Prowincja

On Bracka, one street off the Main Square, Prowincja has been doing the same thing since the nineties: dim lights, wooden tables, candles in the evening, and the best hot chocolate in the city. Not a powder-and-milk job. Real dark chocolate, melted, thick, topped with a cloud of whipped cream. In summer you can sit outside and watch the Bracka foot traffic without being part of it. In winter the inside is better anyway, cramped and warm and smelling of cocoa.

The regulars are students and office workers who have been coming since university. The cakes are homemade, the tarts rotate with the season, and the staff will not rush you. It is the kind of cafe that tourists walk past because there is no one standing on the pavement with a laminated menu. That is the point.

ul. Bracka 3-5, Old Town Mon to Thu 8:00 to 23:00, Fri to Sat 8:00 to midnight, Sun 9:00 to 23:00 $
9.5/10
What I ordered
Hot chocolate + seasonal tart

The chocolate is absurdly thick and the tart changes with the calendar. It felt like a secret, which in the Old Town is worth something.

03

Cafe Camelot

Thirty years on Tomasza and nothing has changed, which is the whole selling proposition. Candles, mismatched wooden furniture, rooms so small you can hear the conversation at the next table, and a menu that has never heard of oat milk or acai bowls. Coffee. Hot chocolate. Apple pie with cream. That is it.

The regulars are artists and academics. The tourists who stumble in tend to stay, because Camelot is doing something the chain cafes across the road cannot fake: atmosphere that took three decades to accumulate. The apple pie alone is worth knowing Tomasza 17 by heart, warm, with cinnamon, served the way your grandmother would if your grandmother had a tenement off the Main Square.

ul. sw. Tomasza 17, Old Town Mon to Thu 9:00 to 23:00, Fri to Sat 9:00 to midnight, Sun 9:00 to 23:00 $
9/10
What I ordered
Apple pie with cream + black coffee

Warm, cinnamoned, old-fashioned. The kind of pie that makes you wonder why anyone bothers with croissants.

04

Massolit Books & Cafe

An English-language bookstore and cafe on Felicjanek, near the western Planty, in a quiet residential street that no tourist itinerary includes. Thousands of titles in English, from Polish literature in translation to obscure American poetry, shelved floor to ceiling. The cafe side does American-style cakes baked on site: pumpkin pie, carrot cake, cheesecake, with mini slices for the indecisive. Coffee comes from a local Krakow roastery.

The crowd is expats, Jagiellonian academics, Erasmus students and the occasional local who reads in English. It is the only place on this list where you might spend more on books than on coffee, which is how bookstore cafes should work.

ul. Felicjanek 4, Old Town (west) Daily from 10:00 to 20:00 $
8.5/10
What I ordered
Carrot cake + filter coffee + a book I will pretend was planned

Dense, properly spiced carrot cake. The book cost more than the food. No regrets on either purchase.

05

Bunkier Cafe

Attached to the Bunkier Sztuki contemporary art gallery on plac Szczepanski, with a garden terrace that sits right in the Planty park. This is the cafe for people who want to be outside without being on the Main Square. The terrace faces the trees, not the trams; the gallery crowd filters in between exhibitions; and the menu does solid coffee and simple food without pretending to be a restaurant.

In summer, the garden is one of the best seats in the Old Town. In winter, the brutalist concrete interior of the gallery itself is worth the visit. Either way, plac Szczepanski is a two-minute walk from Florianska and feels like a different city.

plac Szczepanski 3a, Old Town Mon to Thu 9:00 to midnight, Fri to Sat 9:00 to 2:00, Sun 9:00 to midnight $$
8/10

Americano on the garden terrace

The coffee is decent, the terrace is the draw. Trees above, Planty around, and the Main Square somewhere behind you where it belongs.

06

De Revolutionibus Books & Cafe

An academic bookshop and cafe on Bracka 14, run by the Copernicus Centre Foundation. The name comes from the obvious place, and the shelves match: philosophy, reportage, independent publishers, Copernicus-themed editions and a tight selection of books you will not find in the chain stores on the Main Square.

The cafe side serves specialty coffee and themed drinks (the Copernicus latte is exactly the kind of thing you expect), with a quiet upper floor that students and researchers use as a reading room. It is the second bookshop cafe on this list, after Massolit, and both prove the same point: Krakow has an extraordinary number of places where books and coffee share a room, and those rooms tend to be the ones worth sitting in.

ul. Bracka 14, Old Town Mon to Sat 8:00 to 20:00, closed Sun $
8/10
What I ordered
Espresso + browsing the philosophy shelf

Good coffee, calm room, and a bookshop that makes you feel smarter just for being in it. The upper floor is genuinely quiet.

The pattern

Look at a map and you will notice: most of these sit on Bracka, Felicjanek or plac Szczepanski, the quiet western side of the Old Town. This is not a coincidence. The eastern half, between Florianska and the Main Square, is where the foot traffic concentrates and where rents force cafes to serve volume over quality. One ring west, the streets get residential, the rents drop, and the coffee gets better. The same pattern holds across Krakow: the best food is always one district out from the center, which is why we write guides to Kleparz and Kazimierz as well.

Questions people actually ask me

Is it worth getting coffee in Krakow's Old Town?

Yes, if you know where. The tourist strip around the Main Square charges premium prices for mediocre coffee. Move one or two streets west, toward Krupnicza or Felicjanek, and you find specialty cafes at normal prices with zero tourist markup.

Where is the best hot chocolate in Krakow?

Nowa Prowincja on Bracka 3-5 is the local consensus: real melted dark chocolate, not powder from a packet, topped with whipped cream. It has been the answer for about thirty years.

Are there any bookshop cafes in Krakow?

Massolit Books and Cafe on Felicjanek 4 is the best known: an English-language bookstore with homemade cakes. In Kazimierz, Lokator on Mostowa 1 is a Polish-language bookshop and cafe covered in our Kazimierz guide.

Where can I find specialty coffee in Krakow Old Town?

Bruk Cafe on Krótka 1, five minutes north of the Barbakan in Kleparz, which belongs to the Old Town district: serious espresso, a matcha list longer than most matcha bars and breakfast until 1 pm. Inside the Planty ring, De Revolutionibus Books and Cafe at ul. Bracka 14 also serves specialty coffee in an academic bookshop setting run by the Copernicus Centre.

Can I work from a laptop in Old Town cafes?

Bruk Cafe on Krótka 1, a five-minute walk north of the Barbakan, has sockets within reach of most tables, opens at 8:00 on weekdays and is open on Sunday mornings. It tops our work-from-cafe guide, which has more laptop-tested cafes across districts.
