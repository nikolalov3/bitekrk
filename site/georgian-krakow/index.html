The Best Georgian Food in Krakow

By Cezary Musiał · Published 30 July 2026 · Hours checked October 2026, prices August 2026

Ask a Krakow local for a cheap, generous, crowd-pleasing dinner and there is a good chance they say Georgian. Poland fell hard for the food in the last decade, and the reasons are easy to taste: molten cheese bread, dumplings that feel like a cousin of pierogi, walnut and bean dishes for the table, and famously good wine that costs very little. Kazimierz is the heart of it, and the five below are the ones worth crossing town for.

One honest framing before the list. Georgian dining in Krakow is casual to mid-range. There is no white-tablecloth Georgian restaurant here, so the top end is a full supra feast with a bottle of Saperavi, not a tasting menu. That is a feature, not a flaw. Five places, from a walk-in on the Royal Route to a riverside dumpling house.

Kazimierz · the benchmark

Tbilisuri

The one local food writers keep calling the best Georgian in Krakow, on Meiselsa in the heart of Kazimierz. The kitchen is run by a Georgian chef, the wine is brought in directly from Georgia, and there is a summer garden. It has the highest review count of any Georgian spot in the city, which for a cuisine this crowded is the clearest signal there is. Book on weekends.

ul. Beera Meiselsa 5, Kazimierz Tue to Sun 13:00 to 22:00, closed Mon $$
9.5/10

Adjaruli khachapuri + khinkali + lamb mtsvadi

Georgian chef, wine straight from Georgia, and the review count to back the reputation. The one to beat, and the safest first Georgian meal in the city.

Kazimierz · riverside

Chinkalnia

A dedicated khinkali house right by the Bernatka footbridge, with a modern room and a terrace over the Vistula. Khinkali is the specialty, juicy veal-and-broth dumplings eaten by hand: bite the side, sip the broth, work through the plate, leave the tough top knot. Pair it with chacha, the Georgian grape spirit, if you are feeling brave. The setting is the prettiest of the group.

Kazimierz · home style

Nesi's Home Kitchen

Tiny, roughly two dozen seats, on the food street of Świętego Wawrzyńca, and one of the highest-rated Georgian rooms in the city despite being one of the newest. The pitch is exactly what the name says: eat as if you have wandered into a Georgian grandmother's kitchen. Home-style cooking, a homely room, and baklava to finish with a glass of Georgian wine. Book or come early, because it fills.

ul. Świętego Wawrzyńca 3, Kazimierz Tue to Thu 13:00 to 21:30, Fri to Sat 13:00 to 22:00, Sun 13:00 to 21:30, closed Mon $$
9/10

Khinkali + adjaruli khachapuri + baklava

A tiny home-style kitchen and one of the highest-rated Georgian rooms in town. It feels personal in a way the bigger places cannot. Book the small room ahead.

Kazimierz · wine

Smaki Gruzji

Part restaurant, part winiarnia, on Dietla, and the place to actually taste your way through Georgian wine by the glass rather than committing to a bottle. The kitchen is run by Georgian chefs and the food is honest and well priced: adjaruli, khinkali, lobio, tolma. It is small, so seating can be tight, but for the wine angle it is the pick of the five.

Old Town · central

Gruzińskie Chaczapuri

The most central and longest-running Georgian address, on Grodzka between the Main Square and Wawel, with a second branch on Sienna. It is Polish-owned but with Georgian cooks, and it is the reliable, walk-in, tourist-friendly option when you are in the Old Town and want khachapuri without a trek to Kazimierz. Broad wine list, Georgian lemonade, easy to get into.

How to use this list

First time, go to Tbilisuri and order adjaruli khachapuri and a plate of khinkali, the two dishes everyone should start with. Want the setting, book the riverside terrace at Chinkalnia. Want the wine, sit at Smaki Gruzji. Want it homely, squeeze into Nesi's. Staying in the Old Town and not up for a walk, Gruzińskie Chaczapuri on Grodzka does the job.

Nearly all of it lives in Kazimierz, which is the district to graze Georgian food street by street. If you only have one dinner and want to understand why Poland loves this cuisine, this is where to spend it.

Questions people actually ask

What is the best Georgian restaurant in Krakow?

Tbilisuri on Beera Meiselsa 5 in Kazimierz: start with adjaruli khachapuri and khinkali. Chinkalnia for the river terrace, Smaki Gruzji for the wine, Nesi's Home Kitchen for a small home-style room.

What is adjaruli khachapuri?

The boat-shaped one: a bread canoe filled with molten cheese, topped with a raw egg yolk and a knob of butter that you stir together at the table, then tear off pieces of the crust to dip. It is the single dish to order first at any Georgian restaurant.

Is Georgian food good for vegetarians?

Very. Beyond the cheese breads, order lobio, a spiced bean stew, badrijani, walnut-stuffed eggplant, and pkhali, vegetable and walnut pates. A whole table can eat well without meat, which is part of why the cuisine caught on here.

Do I need to book a Georgian restaurant in Krakow?

Not everywhere, but the small rooms fill: Nesi's Home Kitchen and Smaki Gruzji especially, and Tbilisuri on weekends. Gruzińskie Chaczapuri in the Old Town is the easiest walk-in.
