Kleparz: Where Locals Eat Near Krakow's Oldest Market

By Cezary Musiał · Published 8 August 2026 · Details checked August 2026

Most visitors see Kleparz for exactly four minutes: the walk from the main station to the Barbakan gate. Which is a shame, because the district squeezed between the Planty park and the railway line has been feeding Krakow for roughly eight hundred years, and it still does it better, and cheaper, than half the Old Town.

The heart of it is Stary Kleparz, a covered market on Rynek Kleparski that has been trading since the Middle Ages. Farmers from villages around Krakow sell seasonal vegetables, oscypek and other mountain cheeses, smoked meats and bread that was baked the same morning. It runs Monday to Saturday from early morning, and the earlier you come the better it is. My grandmother bought her tomatoes here. So do I, from what I suspect is the same stall.

Around the market, quietly and without any of the fuss of Kazimierz, a proper little food neighborhood has grown. Here is where I would take you if you gave me a morning in Kleparz, with a few detours worth knowing about on the way.

01

Bruk Cafe

Start here. A small specialty cafe on Krótka, one of those streets you only find because you were looking for something else. Coffee is taken seriously, the matcha menu is longer than in most dedicated matcha bars, and breakfast runs until noon. Five minutes from the station, so it also works as the first or last stop of a trip, and it is open Sunday mornings, which in this part of town is rarer than it should be.

From the doorstep you are two minutes from the Barbakan, the round brick fortress that guarded the medieval city gate, and from St. Florian's Church, where the royal coronation route into the Old Town begins. Have breakfast, cross the street, and you are standing on seven centuries of history before your flat white has cooled.

02

Szklarnia

Walk north up Krowoderska, the long calm street that carries the neighborhood's everyday life, and you reach Szklarnia. The name means greenhouse and they commit to it: coffee served between plants, cut flowers and shelves of books, in a green room that half the neighborhood treats as an extension of their living room. Come for a slow afternoon coffee rather than a quick shot, that is the pace of the place.

03

Kawiarnia Fornir

Down on Długa, closer to the Planty end, Fornir is the kind of cafe Krakow does not build anymore: wood, patience and cake. It is where the neighborhood actually sits, retirees next to students next to someone's overexcited spaniel. Come in the afternoon when the light hits the front windows.

Długa itself rewards a slow walk: it is one of the oldest streets outside the walls, running from the Planty straight into the heart of old Kleparz, lined with the kind of unrestored townhouses that photographers love and developers eye. The sandwiches at Fornir are built from whatever was good at Stary Kleparz that morning, and you can taste the distance from stall to plate: about two hundred meters.

04

Cukiernia BiBułka

Technically a few streets over the district line on Karmelicka, but no honest sweet tooth draws borders. BiBułka is the pastry shop that brought Dubai chocolate to Krakow first, before every second bakery in Poland jumped on the pistachio and kadayif wave, and the queues have not really stopped since. The classic cakes behind the glass deserve attention too.

The walk there is its own reward: Karmelicka is the grand old artery of the Piasek district, ten minutes of art nouveau facades, and on the way you pass the Carmelite church that gave the street its name. Buy the chocolate, walk back toward the market through the quiet grid of side streets, and you have seen a Krakow most visitors never do.

05

Baqaro

The strangest and best thing to happen to the market in years: a tiny Venetian style aperitivo bar wedged in among the stalls of Stary Kleparz. Cicchetti, a short honest wine list, spritz made properly. When the market winds down in the late afternoon and the vegetable crates get stacked, Baqaro is what keeps the square alive into the evening.

How to do Kleparz in one morning

Breakfast at Bruk around nine, before the station crowd works out it exists. Two minutes to the Barbakan for the obligatory look at the walls, then the market while it is loud: buy cheese you do not need, bread you do. A detour up Karmelicka for BiBułka's Dubai chocolate, a slow coffee among the plants at Szklarnia on the way back. If it is past four and the day went well, a spritz at Baqaro before the stalls close around you. Total damage, less than a single dinner on the Main Square, and you covered eight hundred years of the city between courses.

Kleparz is not going to stay overlooked forever. The station keeps growing, the rents inside the Planty keep climbing, and sooner or later everyone follows the cheap tomatoes. Go now and you can still say you were early.

Questions people actually ask me

Is Stary Kleparz open on Sundays?

The market itself trades Monday to Saturday from early morning. On Sundays the square goes quiet, but Bruk Cafe around the corner is open 9:00 to 14:00, which makes it the neighborhood's Sunday plan.

How far is Kleparz from the main station?

Five minutes on foot, and that is walking slowly with a suitcase. Kleparz sits directly between Kraków Główny and the Old Town gates, which is exactly why it works as the first or last stop of a trip.

Cash or card at the market?

Carry cash. Plenty of stalls take cards now, but the small farmers' stands often do not, and those stands are the whole point of coming.

When is the best time to come?

Before ten in the morning, when the produce is fullest and the queues are locals, not tours. Saturdays are the loudest and the best.
