Kleparz: Where Locals Eat Near Krakow's Oldest Market

By Cezary Musiał · Published 8 August 2026 · Details checked August 2026

Most visitors see Kleparz for exactly four minutes: the walk from the main station to the Barbakan gate. Which is a shame, because the district squeezed between the Planty park and the railway line has been feeding Krakow for roughly eight hundred years, and it still does it better, and cheaper, than half the Old Town.

The heart of it is Stary Kleparz, a covered market on Rynek Kleparski that has been trading since the Middle Ages. Farmers from villages around Krakow sell seasonal vegetables, oscypek and other mountain cheeses, smoked meats and bread that was baked the same morning. It runs Monday to Saturday from early morning, and the earlier you come the better it is. My grandmother bought her tomatoes here. So do I, from what I suspect is the same stall.

Around the market, quietly and without any of the fuss of Kazimierz, a proper little food neighborhood has grown. Here is where I would take you if you gave me a morning in Kleparz.

01Bruk Cafe

ul. Krótka 1 Mon to Sat 9:00 to 18:00, Sun 9:00 to 14:00 $$

Start here. A small specialty cafe on Krótka, one of those streets you only find because you were looking for something else. Coffee is taken seriously, the matcha menu is longer than in most dedicated matcha bars, and breakfast runs until noon. Five minutes from the station, so it also works as the first or last stop of a trip.

Open Sunday mornings, which in this part of town is rarer than it should be.

02Tociekawa Specialty Coffee

ul. Długa 37 Mon to Sun from 7:30 $$

The name means "that's interesting" and the queues mean everyone already knows. Tociekawa is the specialty heavyweight of the northern districts: own beans, baristas who will talk brewing methods for as long as you let them, and doors that open at half past seven. If Bruk is your quiet morning, this is your caffeinated errand run.

03Kawiarnia Fornir

ul. Długa 12 Daily 10:00 to 19:00 $$

Lower on Długa, closer to the Planty end, Fornir is the kind of cafe Krakow does not build anymore: wood, patience and cake. It is where the neighborhood actually sits, retirees next to students next to someone's overexcited spaniel. Come in the afternoon when the light hits the front windows.

04Aura Cafe

Stary Kleparz market, Rynek Kleparski Market hours, Mon to Sat $

Inside the market itself, under a sign that says simply KAWA, which is all the menu you need. You drink a genuinely good espresso standing between flower stalls and a man selling smoked cheese, and it costs less than anywhere within the Planty ring. This is my favourite cup of coffee in Krakow and it is not particularly close.

Cash is safer here. It is still a market.

05Baqaro

Stary Kleparz market, Rynek Kleparski Afternoons into the evening $$

The strangest and best thing to happen to the market in years: a tiny Venetian style aperitivo bar wedged in among the stalls. Cicchetti, a short honest wine list, spritz made properly. When the market winds down in the late afternoon, Baqaro is what keeps the square alive.

How to do Kleparz in one morning

Breakfast at Bruk around nine, before the station crowd works out it exists. Walk the market while it is loud: buy cheese you do not need, bread you do. Espresso at Aura standing up, like a local. If it is past four and the day went well, a spritz at Baqaro before the stalls close around you. Total damage, less than a single dinner on the Main Square.

Kleparz is not going to stay overlooked forever. The station keeps growing, the rents inside the Planty keep climbing, and sooner or later everyone follows the cheap tomatoes. Go now and you can still say you were early.
