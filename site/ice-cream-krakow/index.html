Family-Run Ice Cream in Krakow, No Chains Allowed

By Cezary Musiał · Published 6 August 2026 · Details checked August 2026

Krakow has plenty of fashionable ice cream, sold in pastel interiors with single-origin everything. This article is not about that. It is about the other tradition: family workshops that have been freezing cream since before anyone said the word gelato out loud in this city, where the flavor list fits on one hand and the queue is part of the recipe.

One rule for this list: no chains, no franchises, no third location in a shopping mall. Every place here is a family business you can only really experience in one spot. Most of them run seasonally, roughly spring to the first frost, so treat this as a summer document and check before a special trip.

01

Lody na Starowiślnej

The queue on Starowiślna is one of Krakow's most reliable summer landmarks, and it forms for a reason. Stanisław Sarga's pastry workshop has been serving ice cream from the same window, in the same decor, since 1985: cream, cocoa, coffee, blueberry, strawberry, a nut flavor, occasionally wild strawberry when the season allows. That is the whole list, and it is the only Polish lodziarnia ranked in Taste Atlas' world top 100. Bring cash, take the old-style wafer, and eat it walking toward the river. From here you are a few streets from Kazimierz, which is the correct direction.

ul. Starowiślna 83 Season roughly March to mid-November, ~9:00 to 20:00 $
9.5/10

Tastes like 1985 in the best possible way. Four decades of queues cannot all be wrong, and they are not.

02

Lody Tradycyjne Bracia Hodurek

The Hodurek family story sounds invented and is not: an Italian soldier named Salcano gave their great-grandfather a handwritten ice cream recipe in 1917, and the grandson found it in a family trunk almost a century later. The shop on Zwierzyniecka, a few minutes from Wawel, scoops what came out of that trunk: dense, creamy, made from ingredients you can pronounce, with a vegan line that converts skeptics. The family runs a few of its own neighborhood windows around the city, which is not a chain, it is cousins.

9/10
What I ordered
Traditional cream scoop + vegan pistachio

The 1917 recipe holds up against anything scooped in this century. The vegan pistachio is the quiet surprise of the list.

03

Lody U Marysi

On Karmelicka since 1990, U Marysi scoops to family recipes carried from Krościenko nad Dunajcem, in the Pieniny mountains, which might explain why the cream tastes like it has seen an actual meadow. Locals call these "lody prawdziwe", real ice cream, and the phrase does the reviewing for me. If you are following our Kleparz guide up Karmelicka toward BiBułka's Dubai chocolate, you now have a two-stop dessert route and my blessing.

04

Lodowa Huta

Nowa Huta, the planned socialist district a tram ride east of the center, has its own ice cream culture and defends it fiercely. Lodowa Huta is its modern champion: an independent shop on the central estate announcing its rotating flavors on Facebook each morning, meringue with raspberry one day, coffee with rum the next. On warm evenings the line stretches along the block, and half of it is arguing whether Lodowa Huta beats the downtown craft shops. It does, and you get the district's monumental architecture thrown in free.

Crunch, cream and sharp fruit in one scoop. Worth the tram ride on its own, and the tram ride is half the fun.

05

Lody Włoskie Mona

A few estates over, Mona plays the purest version of the old game: soft-serve "lody włoskie", one flavor per day, two sizes, no improvers, no menu anxiety. You eat what the day gives you, which on my visit was vanilla so clean it reset my understanding of vanilla. The one-flavor format is not a limitation, it is a philosophy, and the Sunday queues along the estate prove the neighborhood agrees.

The one-day ice cream route

Start at U Marysi on Karmelicka before the day heats up, walk through the Planty to Bracia Hodurek under Wawel, then cross into Kazimierz and finish at the Starowiślna queue, which by afternoon is at full theatrical length. If you still mean business, tram 4 or 10 takes you to Nowa Huta for Lodowa Huta and Mona before closing. Five family businesses, one day, roughly the price of a single dessert in a Main Square restaurant.

Questions people actually ask me

Which ice cream in Krakow is the most famous?

The Starowiślna 83 window, the Sarga family workshop open since 1985. The queue is the landmark, the shortlist of flavors is the menu, and Taste Atlas ranks it among the world's hundred best.

How much is a scoop?

At the family shops, roughly 3 to 5 zloty. The fashionable places in the center charge two or three times that. Starowiślna is cash only, so have coins ready.

Are these places open in winter?

Mostly no. The traditional lodziarnie run from early spring to October or November; the Starowiślna workshop famously switches to doughnuts for the cold months. Plan ice cream trips between April and October.

What about Good Lood and the craft shops?

Perfectly decent, and deliberately not in this article. This list is for single-family businesses you cannot find in a mall, which is exactly what makes them worth a detour.
