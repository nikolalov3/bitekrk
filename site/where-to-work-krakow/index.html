Best Cafes to Work From a Laptop in Krakow

By Cezary Musiał · Published 8 August 2026 · Hours and details checked August 2026

Krakow is full of beautiful cafes where you absolutely cannot work. Tiny marble tables, one socket hidden behind the fridge, a barista who starts stacking chairs at four. I have been thrown out of enough of them, politely and less politely, to know the difference.

So instead of a list of twenty places I half-remember, here are four I keep coming back to, tested the only way that counts: a full working day in each, laptop open, deadline real. For every one I am telling you what I ordered, whether it was worth it, and what the streets around it are like, because a good work cafe is also a good base for the day.

01

Bruk Cafe, Kleparz

Five minutes on foot from the main station, on the quiet side of Kleparz, and somehow still not overrun. Proper specialty coffee, a matcha menu longer than in most dedicated matcha bars, breakfast until noon, and sockets within reach of most tables, which in Krakow counts as luxury. I have written half of this website at their window table.

The location is the quiet trick. Krótka is a side street between the Barbakan and the Stary Kleparz market, so when you close the laptop you are two minutes from the medieval walls in one direction and from Krakow's oldest food market in the other. If your day has a gap, walk it off along the Planty ring, the green belt that circles the Old Town where the city walls used to stand, and be back before your coffee goes cold.

My pick if you have a train to catch later and a deadline before it. And if the working day stretches, breakfast until noon means you never have to move.

02

I Love Coffee, Krowodrza

A family-run cafe out on Kazimierza Wielkiego, past the point where tourist maps give up, which is exactly why the tables are free. Coffee done both classic and filter, homemade breakfasts and lunches so you never have to pack up mid-task, plants everywhere and air conditioning that actually works in August. By the third visit the staff remembered my order, which has never once happened to me inside the Planty ring.

Krowodrza itself is Krakow at its most normal, in the best sense: tram stops, bakeries, people walking dogs that know the route better than they do. You are ten minutes by tram from the Old Town but paying residential district prices, and after work the Młynówka Królewska park path runs green through the whole neighborhood if you need to walk the screen out of your eyes.

03

Fitagain Coffee & Food, Old Town

The rare Old Town cafe that actually wants you to work there: a large common table, a bar along the window and more wall sockets than some coworking spaces. Two minutes from the Main Square, so it is also the answer when someone insists on meeting somewhere central and you still have emails to send. Lunchtime gets lively; mornings are gold.

Szczepańska runs straight off the Main Square, which means your coffee breaks come with scenery: plac Szczepański with its art nouveau facades and the Szołayski House museum is thirty seconds away, and the Planty park entrance is at the end of the street. Work until one, then join the city: this is the only cafe on this list where the tourist route is a feature, not a bug.

04

Wesoła Immersive, Wesoła

A new media art center with a cafe attached, in the old hospital quarter east of the Old Town that the city is slowly turning into something interesting. Big space, nobody hovering, open until nine in the evening. On a weekday afternoon it might be the quietest room with coffee in central Krakow, and when your eyes need a break from the spreadsheet, there is literal art to stare at.

Wesoła is worth a wander in itself: the Kopernika street corridor of century-old clinic buildings and gardens, the Botanical Garden of the Jagiellonian University at the far end, and a fraction of the foot traffic of anywhere west of it. This is where Krakow will be cool in five years. For now it is where Krakow is quiet, which for a working afternoon is better.

A few honest rules for working in Krakow cafes

Order something every ninety minutes or so. Take calls outside. If the place is filling up for lunch, give up the four-person table without being asked. Do this and Krakow will let you work almost anywhere; skip it and you are the reason the next cafe puts up a no-laptops sign, and I will have to rewrite this article.

Questions people actually ask me

Can you work from a laptop in Krakow cafes at all?

Yes, but choose wisely. Plenty of Old Town cafes limit laptop hours or ban screens at weekends, and nobody puts that on a sign outside. The four places above welcome working guests, that is why they are the whole list.

Which cafe is best if I am near the main station?

Bruk Cafe, without hesitation. Five minutes on foot from Kraków Główny, sockets at most tables, breakfast until noon. I have caught a train with a warm cheesecake in my bag more than once.

How long can I actually sit there?

The unwritten Krakow contract: one order per ninety minutes buys you the seat. Keep to it and a full working day is fine. Break it and you will feel the barista's eyes on your neck, deservedly.

Cafe or coworking, what is cheaper?

A coworking day pass runs 60 to 90 zloty in Krakow. A cafe day is two coffees and a cake, 40 to 60 zloty, and the food is better. Coworking wins only if you need calls, meetings or a monitor.
